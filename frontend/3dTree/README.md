# Ketapang River | Stage 4

A self-contained web scene for the ESG Centre tree screen. The centrepiece is the full-grown Ketapang exported from the refined Blender tree, with its bark and leaf-vein textures embedded.

## Run on Windows

1. Extract the entire ZIP and open the extracted folder in VS Code.
2. In the project, start the servers (`node startAll.js`), set Admin → Digital Tree Adjustment → Display 2D/3D Tree to **3D Tree**, and open `/3dTree`. On its own, open `3dTree.html` with **Live Server** (remove its `<base href="/3dTree/">` line first). With Python installed, you can also run `py -m http.server 8000` in the extracted folder and visit `http://localhost:8000/`.
3. The opening view centres the Ketapang, with the river, banks and forest visible across the 7 m × 2 m display. **Enable sound** starts the ambience. **Wind now** previews a gust.

On a wall-shaped screen such as the 7 m x 2 m display (3.5:1), the camera moves closer and aims a little lower so the tree fills the height as the main focus (`wallDistance` and `wallAimDrop` in `tree-view.js`), and the panels move to the sides (the `min-aspect-ratio: 12/5` rule at the end of `scene.css`). Ordinary screens keep the original view.

**Year Review.** The bottom-right button opens the same year review book as the 2D tree (`year-review.js`, styles at the end of `scene.css`): turn through the years, see each year's leaf count, and **View Tree** shows that year's review in the heading; **Live** returns to the live tree. It reads `/api/tree/years` and `/api/tree?year=`.

**Admin settings.** Admin > Digital Tree Adjustment > 3D Tree Settings sets the weather (live or a manual time of day and rain), the rain area, the river level, the slow orbit, sound and volume, the soil cutaway, which on-screen panels are shown, and the title box (text, opacity, blur, corners, padding, position). `tree3d-settings.js` reads them from `/api/parameters` as soon as the admin saves (the live connection, `live-updates.js`), or every 5 seconds without it and applies a change by operating the page's own controls, so hidden panels still work and someone using the on-screen controls is not overruled until the next change. "Wind gust now" and "Play sunset now" run once on the screen.

**View and zoom.** The admin's 3D View & Zoom card shows a live preview of this page (`?viewEditor=1`: no automatic turn, no sound, zooming on) to turn round the tree and zoom in or out (the mouse wheel, a pinch, the + and - keys or the card's buttons, from 0.5 to 2.5 times the standard framing: `ZOOM_MIN`, `ZOOM_MAX` in `tree-view.js`), save the view as a named snapshot (up to 12, `tree3dParameters.savedViews`: zoom, angle round the tree and height), load a saved one back into the preview, and choose which the 3D screens show (`screenView`, or the standard view). The screens open at the chosen view and move smoothly to a newly chosen one within about 5 seconds; the automatic turn carries on from it, keeping its zoom and height (`view-settings.js`). Viewers cannot zoom, so every screen shows the view the admin chose.

**Ambient sounds and background music.** The admin chooses the sounds in 3D Scene Controls > Sound (all under the one sound switch and volume: `ambience.js`). **Ambient sounds**, each with its own tickbox and volume (0 to 100, within the Volume): River water (the flowing river, a recording; `ambientRiver`, on), Birds (a magpie-robin calling now and then, quieter at night and in rain; `ambientBirds`, on), Forest wind (the breeze, stronger in gusts; `ambientWind`, on), Gentle rain (a soft, steady rain whatever the weather; `ambientRain`, off) and Night insects (crickets and cicadas, made by the page, a little louder at night; `ambientInsects`, off), each with its `…Volume`; the weather's own sounds (rain as it rains, thunder, a leaf's splash) play as well. **Background music** in the chosen style (`musicStyle`; None turns it off: `musicEnabled`), at its Music volume (`musicVolume`, 45): Calm kalimba (`kalimba`, the default: soft kalimba notes in C major pentatonic over a warm pad and a low bass, 62 beats a minute, through C, A minor, F and G), Soft piano (`piano`: a slow, quiet piano tune with a gently broken chord in the left hand, through F, G, E minor and A minor), Dreamy pads (`dreamy`: long, slowly swelling chords with a few far-off bell notes, 46 beats a minute) or Music box (`musicbox`: a light music box rippling through the chords, high and delicate). Every style is original music composed live by the page (`background-music.js` `MUSIC_STYLES`, Web Audio), in a hall's echo; the tune is chosen as it plays, by small steps through the chord's notes, with rests, so it never repeats exactly, and there is no recording or anyone else's music in it, nothing to license; changing the style fades one into the other. Or **My uploaded music** (`musicStyle` `uploaded`): the admin uploads their own music file (MP3, OGG, WAV, M4A, AAC, WebM or FLAC, up to 30 MB) under the music style; it is saved in `assets/Tree/music` (`POST /api/admin/parameters/tree3d-music/upload`, listed and deleted with `.../tree3d-music/list` and `DELETE .../tree3d-music/:filename`) and played at once, looped, instead of the composed music (`musicTrack`), with the ambient sounds still playing (untick them for only that music); if it cannot be played (deleted, or a format the browser does not play) the Calm kalimba plays instead. The admin's Listen here plays the 3D screen's own sound engine with the choices on the form, so it sounds exactly as the screen will, and follows every change while it plays. As with all sound in a browser, it starts by itself once the page has been touched (or in a browser allowed to autoplay); until then the page shows "Tap anywhere to turn on the sound". To have all the sound start by itself every time the page loads, apply `scripts/browser/allow-3d-tree-autoplay.reg` once on the 3D screen's PC (double-click, answer Yes: it needs administrator rights): the Chrome and Edge AutoplayAllowlist policy for localhost, 127.0.0.1 and the PC's network address, any port; `undo-3d-tree-autoplay.reg` removes it. Chrome then says it is "managed by your organization" in its menu.

**Sound.** With the admin's sound on (`soundEnabled`), the page starts the river, wind, bird and weather sounds itself. Browsers play sound only after a first touch on the page (unless the kiosk's Chrome is started with `--autoplay-policy=no-user-gesture-required`, when it starts at once): then the page does not wait (`ambience.js` gives up on the suspended audio after 1.5 seconds, `SoundBlocked`), the sound button reads "Tap to start sound", a note at the bottom says "Tap anywhere to turn on the sound" (`#sound-hint`), and the next touch, click or key press anywhere on the page starts it, also with the controls bar hidden (`tree3d-settings.js` `armSoundRetry`); once it has started, someone muting it on screen is not overruled. The admin's **Listen here** (3D Scene Controls > Sound) plays the same river and bird sounds in the admin's browser at the chosen volume.

**Loading.** While the page loads, only the loading card shows, on a plain background: the tree, the title box, Year Review and the panels stay hidden (`scene.css`, until `.reserve` has `data-ready`). `scene.js` shows them all at once, fading in from behind a curtain, when the scene is ready: its shaders compiled (so it does not stutter as it appears), the admin's settings applied (so the title box has its own text), the visitors' leaves on the tree (`feedback-leaves.js` `ready`) and the fonts loaded; after `REVEAL_WAIT_MS` (20 seconds) it shows whatever is ready, so a slow part never keeps it on the loading card.

**Glowing look.** `glow-look.js` gives the scene the look of the ESG Centre's glowing-tree concept: the river valley is held at night in cool moonlight (after the weather's own update every frame, so live weather and the sky settings never brighten it); everything glows in the three ESG colours, green (Environmental), blue (Social) and orange (Governance): streams of green, blue and orange light run along all the wood, up the trunk, out along every branch and along the roots, with pulses travelling out along them (each a bright line in a soft halo of its colour, `STREAM_WIDTH` wide and `STREAM_HALO` times that for the halo; the admin turns them on or off and sets their Strength, thinner and dimmer at 50% to thicker and brighter at 200%: 3D Scene Controls > Look, "Trunk glow", `trunkGlow`, `trunkGlowStrength`, applied without reloading by `setTrunkGlow`, fading over `TRUNK_GLOW_FADE`; off shows the plain bark in the moonlight) (`BARK_GLOW` in `glow-look.js`: an orange, a blue and a green line at their own places round each trunk, branch and root, from the model's texture coordinates, whose u goes round each piece of wood and v along it, so the lines follow every branch and root whichever way it grows; `STREAM_WIDTH`); the tree's own leaves glow, each leaf in its own vivid colour, green, blue or orange (about 45%, 30% and 25%: `LEAF_SHARES`; each leaf's own random number `leafPick`, from the leaf as one piece of the foliage), lit from within with an edge of light and glowing veins, a few twinkling; their light, the light streams' and the rings' spill round them in a soft glow (a bloom, `createBloom`: everything on `GLOW_LAYER` is drawn again at a third of the screen size on black, its brighter parts blurred and added onto the frame); rings of light lie on the ground round the base of the tree with a pulse spreading across them (there are no floating specks of light round the canopy); the feedback leaves glow too, each in the admin's colour for its visitor's badge (Ocean Guardian blue, Climate Champion teal, Social Champion amber and so on, as the 2D tree; light while new, a dark shade later: `badgeColour` and `tintLeaf` in `feedback-leaves.js`), or, with "Colour leaves by their badge" off, green, blue or orange by the badge's E, S or G (`ESG_GLOW_COLOURS`, `BADGE_ESG`), with handwritten names (`feedback-leaves.js` LEAF_LOOKS). A VIP's gold leaf has a star of light twinkling at its tip, in both looks, unless the admin turns it off (3D VIP Leaf Adjustments: "White star twinkling at the tip of VIP leaves", `vipSparkle`, on by default); on the tree it also turns gently from left to right and back on its stem, as the viewer sees it, 40° each way over 6 seconds so its name stays readable, each VIP leaf at its own moment, starting gently once it has settled (`VIP_TURN_ANGLE`, `VIP_TURN_SECONDS`, `VIP_TURN_EASE_IN`; "VIP leaves turn gently from left to right", `vipTurn`, on by default) (`LEAF_TIP`, measured from the leaf picture's outline; `SPARKLE_SIZE`, `SPARKLE_EVERY`); in the glowing look the gold leaf is darker, with a gentler gold pulse and gold glow, so its name reads clearly on it (`VIP_GLOWING` in `feedback-leaves.js`: the leaf at 62%, the pulse up to 5% brighter, the gold glow up to 25%; the natural look keeps the 2D tree's pulse). The admin turns it on or off with "Glowing look" in 3D Tree Settings > 3D Scene Controls (`tree3dParameters.glowLook`, on by default); off shows the daylight river valley with natural leaves and bold names. The page reads the setting when it opens (`startingLook`) and reloads into the other look within about 5 seconds of a change. `?look=natural` or `?look=glow` in the page address fixes one, to try it out.

**Feedback leaves.** `feedback-leaves.js` puts one leaf per feedback of the year on the tree, using the RainTree leaf model in `/assets/Tree/3dTreeLeaves/`, with the 2D tree's leaf behaviour: each leaf gets its own spot on the outer canopy (random, kept between refreshes, never overlapping) and its own angle; new feedbacks' leaves arriving together are split into groups as the admin sets (3D Tree Visualization Parameters > Split new leaves arriving together, `leafBatchMode`): into a number of groups (`leafBatchCount`, 1 to 10, 3 by default: 10 new feedbacks into 2 groups are 5 and 5, into 3 groups 4, 3 and 3) or into groups of up to a number of leaves (`leafBatchSize`: 10 in groups of up to 3 are 3, 3, 2 and 2), as evenly as they go, with an example under the setting: a group appears together in a row at the bottom of the screen, below the tree, where a new leaf has always shown, clear of the title box and the Year Review button, the leaves spread evenly over its width whatever the screen's size, all the same size, 1.4 times their size on the tree (SHOWCASE_SIZE; smaller on a narrow screen, so they never overlap), back to their own size early in the float up (SHRINK_SHARE), and stays for the admin's time (12 seconds by default, SHOWCASE_MS), drifting gently with their names level so the visitors can find and read them; then the leaves join the tree in no set order, each leaving at its own moment within 2.5 seconds (BATCH_SPREAD_MS), floating up to their places like falling leaves in reverse (swinging gently from side to side and rocking like a cradle, the swings dying away as they shrink and settle onto their twigs); the next group appears once that one has left (STAGE_CLEAR_MS). To demonstrate it without submitting anything, open the 3D tree with `?demo=7` (for example `http://localhost:3003/3dTree?demo=7`; 1 to 30): the page opens without the 7 newest feedbacks (made up by pretend visitors, "Demo visitor 1" and on, when there are fewer, as after a fresh start) and, 5 seconds after the scene appears, they arrive together as new ones, in groups of 3 (in that browser tab only; nothing is changed; reload to see it again); leaves are placed where the viewer sees them, on screen and not under the title box; a new visitor's leaf glows green for a minute; each leaf shows its visitor's name as on the 2D tree (lying on the leaf, centred in the middle of its blade and along its midrib so it turns with the leaf, wrapped inside the blade in up to three lines of whole words with "…" when too long, the same font size on every leaf, never upside down or mirrored; the admin can turn names off); every leaf is drawn on top of the foliage so the tree's own leaves never cover it (nearer leaves over farther ones, never overlapping each other as the viewer sees them; the leaves stay on the side of the tree facing the viewer, and when the tree turns, by the automatic turn, dragging or the arrow keys, they glide smoothly with it round the trunk, keeping to the canopy's surface and facing the viewer; a leaf stays fully shown until it has glided 90° to one side, the edge of the tree's outline (`GLIDE_REACH`), and then goes out of sight there within a few degrees (`GLIDE_FADE`, 5°); leaves that would cover each other as seen (two at different depths of the canopy lining up as the tree turns) are eased apart across the view every frame, just far enough to stand clear, keeping inside the canopy's outline, and ease back to their spots when nothing is in the way (`spreadApart`: `SPREAD_GAP`, `SPREAD_MAX`, `SPREAD_EASE`); a VIP's leaf always joins the tree at one end of the canopy, its left or its right as the viewer sees it (`sideSpots`: 50° to 80° round from the viewer, in the middle of the canopy's height), and a leaf made VIP while on the tree moves there, then glides and comes back on the other side like the others and comes back in at the same distance on the other side, so the front always has its leaves, all moving with the tree; with the view still, every leaf is exactly at its spot: `shownSpot` in `feedback-leaves.js`), is never darker than a set lightness so it stands out on the canopy, and glows like a leaf of light, as in the ESG Centre's glowing-tree concept: a vivid badge colour (at least NEON_SATURATION saturated), brightest in the middle of the blade, with glowing veins (found from the leaf model's vein relief and the vein lines of its picture), a bright outline in the leaf's own colour (green, blue or orange, never white: `vivid` in GLOW_FRAGMENT, its colour at full brightness), a soft glow around it in its own colour too that breathes gently (AURA), and now and then a light sweeping across it (SHIMMER_EVERY, SHIMMER_SWEEP), while its name is white handwriting (Monotype Corsiva, else Segoe Script) with a soft glow (GLOW_FRAGMENT in feedback-leaves.js); changing the leaf scale spaces the leaves out again for the new size; it shows the same leaves as the 2D tree (the 2D Maximum Visible Leaves newest, none at growth stage 0, all of a reviewed year), so past that count the oldest leaf falls; and Year Review's View Tree shows that year's leaves. New feedback comes at once: `live-updates.js` keeps one connection to `/api/tree/events` and the page fetches `/api/tree` the moment the server says the tree's data changed (see `docs/features/tree.md`); the timed check (every 5 seconds) is then a safety net every 2 minutes, and runs as before while the connection is down. The admin's 3D Leaf Adjustments set the new and older leaf colours, how long a leaf counts as new, the leaf scale, and how the names are written (Names on the leaves: a font style, `leafNameFont`: Match the look, Elegant script, Handwriting, Modern, Rounded, Classic serif or Typewriter, all fonts Windows has; a weight, `leafNameWeight`, Regular to Extra bold; any colour, `leafNameColor`, white by default; and a font size, `leafNameSize`, 70% to 140% of the standard, larger names wrapping onto more lines inside the leaf and a long name ending with "…"; `NAME_FONTS` and `useNameStyle` in `feedback-leaves.js`, with a preview in the admin). "Match the look" is the look's own: handwriting when glowing, clean letters when natural. Light letters glow in their own colour over a soft dark shadow in the glowing look; dark letters, and every name in the natural look, are crisp with an edge (a thin light one for dark letters, so they stay dark). A change is written onto every leaf within about 5 seconds, without reloading. A VIP's leaf (a name in the admin's VIP Management, as `/api/tree` marks it or as `/api/tree/vip-names` lists it) is gold with the 2D tree's gold pulse; the admin's 3D VIP Leaf Adjustments set its gold, its scale and the glow, and, with "Give VIP names their own style" ticked, how VIP names are written: their own font style, weight, size and colour (`vipNameOwnStyle`, `vipNameFont`, `vipNameWeight`, `vipNameColor`, `vipNameSize`; the same choices as the other names, with a preview on the gold leaf; off by default, so VIP names are written as the others; `useNameStyle` in `feedback-leaves.js` keeps the two styles), and a change to the VIP list shows within about 5 seconds (at once in the same browser, through the `tree-vip-refresh` signal).

A local or hosted HTTP server loads the bundled models. The scene uses WebGL 2. Manual weather works offline when the scene is served locally. Live Singapore needs internet access to the weather feeds.


## Leaf placement and animation

The admin's 3D Tree Visualization Parameters are the 2D tree's Visualization Parameters for the 3D tree (saved in `tree3dParameters`, used by `feedback-leaves.js`): the leaf area (how much of the canopy's width and height leaves may sit on, and moved up or down; 2D: Leaf Canopy Width, Height and Top Offset), the leaf refresh interval (how often new feedback is checked, 5 seconds by default), leaf opacity, how long a new leaf takes to fade in, stays at the bottom middle (12 seconds) and floats up (7.2 seconds), how long the oldest leaf takes to fall (4.2 seconds), and Maximum Visible Leaves (shared with the 2D tree). Maximum Visible Leaves is first in, first out: at 100, the 101st feedback joins and then the oldest leaf falls: the new leaf first shows at the bottom and floats up, and only once it has joined the tree does the oldest leaf drop (one for each new leaf, oldest first: `fallQueue`, `letOldestFall`); with no new leaf on its way (a lower Maximum Visible Leaves, a bare trunk) the leaves fall at once. The falling leaf drops smoothly like a real leaf: it lets go gently, glides down in one wide curve out from the tree (no zigzag), rocking softly and turning slowly, and settles on the ground as it fades; when more leaves are on the tree than fit side by side on the canopy at their size, they are all made just small enough to fit. A leaf joins light and turns dark after "A leaf stays light for" (3D Leaf Adjustments, in minutes or hours, for example 1 hour), fading over a few seconds: light green to dark green, or, with badge leaf styling on (on by default; a badge left at its built-in 3D colour takes the 2D tree's Badge Leaf Styling colour), a light shade of its badge's colour to a dark shade of it, as on the 2D tree (light for its first 24 hours, then dark). VIP leaves stay gold.

## Clear of the title box

Feedback leaves never sit under the title box, wherever the admin puts it (top or bottom, left, centre or right) or however big it is: new leaves are only placed clear of it (with a small margin), a new leaf waiting at the bottom middle shows just above a title box placed at the bottom, and when the title box moves or changes size, any leaf it then covers moves to a free spot and fades in there. The same holds on the 2D tree (`tree.js` getTitleKeepOut).

## Leaf details

Click (or tap) a feedback leaf to open its details card, as on the 2D tree: whether the name is shown or hidden for privacy, the visitor's name (or Anonymous visitor), their badge in its colour, the date and their pledge snippet, all from `/api/tree`. The card (`leaf-detail.js`, `#leafDetailCard`) opens beside the leaf on the side with more room, and the leaf grows a little while it is open. Only the leaf shape counts as a click (not the square around it), and where leaves overlap, the one drawn on top; a press that moves turns the view instead. The card closes with its × button, the Escape key, a click away from the leaves, a Year Review change, when the leaf falls, or as soon as the view is turned (a drag, the arrow keys or the slow orbit), since the leaf then moves away from it.

## Pictures for the 2D tree

The 2D tree's Natural and Digital styles show pictures of this tree (checklist item 12). `?capture2d=1` with `?look=natural` or `glow`, `?stage=0` to `4` and `?growth=1` shows the tree still from the front, a little further out than the standard view (`CAPTURE_ZOOM` in `capture-2d.js`), in clear weather with the river at its normal level, without the visitors' leaves, birds or falling leaves, and without the admin's live weather, sound or actions (`tree3d-settings.js`); it stays on this page even when the wall shows the 2D tree. Once ready (`window.tree2dCaptureReady`), `window.tree2dCapture()` returns the frame (1920 x 1080 JPEG, with the glowing look's bloom), a canopy mask (PNG, opaque where the tree's foliage is seen: the frame drawn again without the foliage, `alphaTest` meshes, and compared) and its description. The admin's "Capture from the 3D tree" (2D Tree Display) and `scripts/tree/capture-2d-from-3d.js` save them to `assets/Tree/2d-from-3d/`; see `docs/features/tree.md`.

## Growth stage

The tree grows in the same five stages as the 2D tree: 0 Bare Trunk, 1 Early Growth, 2 Young Canopy, 3 Mature Canopy and 4 Full Canopy. It is the same setting as the 2D tree's (`treeParameters.treeStage`), set in the admin's 3D Tree Growth Stage (or the 2D Tree Display), so both trees always match. In **Auto** (`treeStageMode: 'auto'`) the tree grows by itself: the server reports the stage for this year's leaf count in `/api/parameters` (`backend/features/parameters/treeStage.js`), so when the count reaches a stage's minimum (for example 10 leaves for stage 2) both trees move to that stage within about 15 seconds. Between the stages the 3D tree grows a little with every leaf (`tree-growth.js`): the server also reports how far the tree is from this stage's number to the next (`treeStageAuto.progress`, 0 to 1), and the tree's own foliage fills in with it, spreading out from the middle of the canopy with a ragged edge, each sprig unfolding from nothing over about 9 seconds (`GROW_SECONDS`), without reloading the page; new growth (and the youngest foliage just inside it, `FRESH_REACH`) is a fresh lime green at the leaf's own brightness, a little lighter (so it never glows at night), fading into the canopy over 20 seconds (`FRESH_SECONDS`), so even one leaf's small growth shows. In the natural look the tree is shaded for the time of day as the river and the forest are (`matte-tree.js` timeOfDay; its materials take no lighting of their own): cool moonlight at night, warmer at dusk, a little duller in rain; the feedback leaves are in moonlight at night too, but less dim so their names stay readable (`NIGHT_LEAF`). The glowing look lights the tree itself (`clock.treeShade` 0). Nothing the tree has grown is taken away, and the visitors' leaves stay. When the tree reaches a new stage (in Auto as the leaves come in, or a higher stage saved in the admin), it grows into it in place, without reloading the page (`scene.js` changeStage): the new stage's model loads in the background while the tree stays on show, then takes the tree's place at the tree's size (`matchingScale`) and showing as much foliage as the tree had (the share of the new model's sprigs the stage before had at its fullest: stage 1 a few sprigs, stage 2 about 38%, stage 3 about 75%, from the sprig counts of the models), then grows to its own size with a growth spurt: `STAGE_SPURT` (30%) of the rest of its canopy grows in, fresh green, over `GROW_IN_SECONDS` (12). The visitors' leaves stay where they are; once it has grown, the birds' perches, the wind's falling leaves and the spots for new leaves come from the new tree. A step down (a threshold raised in the admin) changes in place too, the tree shrinking to the smaller stage's size. At stage 4's number (the admin's "Stage 4 (Full Canopy) min leaves") the tree is fully grown. The feedback leaves and the leaves the wind drops use only the foliage grown in so far. In **Manual** the tree stays at the chosen stage, shown full. `tree-stage.js` loads that stage's model, `/assets/Tree/3dTreeLeaves/Ketapang_Stage_0.glb` to `Ketapang_Stage_4.glb` (stage 4 is the same tree as `ketapang-stage-4.glb`, which is also the fallback if a stage's model cannot be loaded). Stages 0 to 3 were exported smaller, so they are scaled up (`YOUNG_STAGE_SCALE`) to grow towards stage 4, and their own patch of soil is left out. The models' trunks are oval (stage 4's about 2 m thick seen from the side but only 1.3 m from the front or back; the young stages' a little so), so every stage's trunk is made round as it loads (`roundTrunk` in `tree-stage.js`): the trunk is measured in bands up its height and its thinner side widened to its thicker side's width, from above the buttress roots (which stay as modelled) to where it ends in the crown. The whole tree is reshaped together, so the branches stay joined, and the widening fades out within `TRUNK_REACH` trunk radii, so the branches and canopy keep their shape (no canopy leaf moves more than about 2 cm). Its canopy is then shaped to look right from every side as the view turns (`canopy-shape.js`): in the models the leaves crowd at the branch tips at the canopy's two ends, so from the side the tree was a wall of leaves, while the canopy's front and back (its outline from the side, where the feedback leaves sit) had about a third as many leaves for their size, so feedback leaves there seemed to float beside the tree. So some leaves are dropped in the canopy's middle from front to back (`THIN_MIDDLE`, what the side view looks straight through) and deep inside it (`THIN_INSIDE`), so the trunk and branches show from the side, the outside of the crown's top keeping all its leaves; the low-hanging underside is trimmed (`TRIM_FROM` to `TRIM_TO`, 90% of the lowest leaves gone), higher under the canopy's two long ends (`TRIM_ENDS_FROM`, `TRIM_ENDS_TO`: from the side the nearer end is closest to the viewer and shows lowest), so from the side the crown ends tidily above the trunk; and the outside of the canopy's front and back gets more leaves (up to `FILL_SIDES` copies of each leaf there, moved a little across and out and turned its own way), so the outline from the side is full and the feedback leaves sit among leaves. Each leaf is kept, dropped or copied whole by a fixed pattern, so the tree is the same at every load; the work is spread over frames when the tree grows into a new stage in place. The birds perch on the nearest branches of the stage shown, the wind drops leaves from its canopy (none from the bare trunk), and the feedback leaves sit on its canopy (none at stage 0: going down to it they fall off; a new leaf that arrives on the bare trunk waits and flies in once the Stage 1 tree is in). On a stage change each feedback leaf moves to the same part of the new canopy, gliding there, and grows out with the tree (feedback-leaves.js `moveLeavesTo`, `followTree`). When the admin saves a different stage, the tree changes to it in place within about 5 seconds (plus the model's loading time). Add `?stage=0` to `?stage=4` to the address to try a stage without changing the setting, and `&growth=0` to `&growth=1` to see it part grown (for example `?stage=2&growth=0.5`), or `&growFrom=` to see it just reached, with its growth spurt (for example `?stage=3&growFrom=2`).

## Tree view

The camera keeps one fixed, close framing with the tree as the main focus: on a laptop screen the canopy fills the width, and on the 3.5:1 wall the tree fills the height with the river and hills either side (frameHeight and frameWidth in `tree-view.js`). Zooming is off (no scroll, pinch or **+**/**−**), so every screen always shows the tree at this size. Drag on the scene or use the arrow keys to orbit the tree.

The automatic turn moves the camera round the tree while the scene is visible: one full turn a day (15° per hour) by default, or as fast as the admin sets it in 3D Scene Controls > Motion ("One full turn every", 30 seconds to 24 hours: `tree3dParameters.orbitSeconds`; "Direction", the tree turning to the right or to the left: `orbitDirection`; `setOrbit` in `tree-view.js`, applied by `scene.js` within about 5 seconds). The turn button shows the time for one turn (for example **5 min turn**) and turns it off or on. It pauses during camera input and resumes a few seconds afterward. It starts on unless reduced motion is enabled. Select the button to toggle it.

## Weather and sky

- **Live Singapore** starts automatically and displays the local time and sky period alongside the rain status. The sky moves through **Sunrise**, **Day**, **Sunset** and **Night** as Singapore time advances.
- **All Singapore** enables rain when any gauge in the latest [NEA rainfall report](https://data.gov.sg/datasets/d_6580738cdd7db79374ed3152159fbd69/view) records rain. The readout shows the number of wet gauges. NEA publishes readings every five minutes, and the scene checks for a new report every minute.
- **Rain area** lets you select a named Singapore area, including Jurong West, Woodlands and Yishun. The scene uses the closest reporting gauge to that area's reference point. The wider area list comes from [NEA's area metadata](https://data.gov.sg/datasets/d_3f9e064e25005b0e42969944ccaf2e7a/view). If the area list is temporarily unavailable, the three built-in areas and any named rain gauges are available.
- Choose **Use my location** and allow location access to follow the nearest rain gauge as you move. The detected area name is approximate, and location coordinates stay in your browser. Use HTTPS or localhost for browser location access. You can select an area directly if location access is unavailable.
- The readout marks missing or stale readings as unavailable. When recent rain data is unavailable, the sky follows the Singapore clock and the scene stays dry.
- [Open-Meteo](https://open-meteo.com/en/docs) supplies local sunrise and sunset times, cloud cover and weather codes. The scene uses approximate local sunrise and sunset times while that feed is unavailable.
- **Manual** gives you **Sunrise**, **Day**, **Sunset** and **Night**, with a gentle 16-second fade when you switch. **Play sunset** takes about 42 seconds from daylight through sunset to moonlit night. If you start it after dark, the scene first eases into daylight through sunrise. The **Rain** button works with any manual sky, including at night.
- Manual rain includes lightning and delayed thunder. Live mode adds thunder when the weather feed reports thunderstorms alongside rain. Enable sound to hear the thunder. The system's reduced-motion setting suppresses bright lightning pulses.
- Two cloud layers drift at different speeds through daylight, sunrise, sunset, night and rain.
- At night, soft moonlight brings out the island, banks and forest floor, and the river takes on a brighter blue tone.

## River level

The **River level** panel shows the simulated water height relative to the island bank mark. At 100%, the water reaches that mark. The river surface rises over the lower banks and drops again as the reading changes. Fish, ducks and floating leaves follow the surface.

| Level | Modeled bank height |
| --- | --- |
| Normal | Below 70% |
| High | 70% to below 90% |
| Danger | 90% and above |

**Auto (rain)** responds gradually to the scene's rain intensity and drains after the rain eases. Use **Preview Normal**, **Preview High** or **Preview Danger** to show each level during a demonstration. The scale and thresholds are for the scene simulation. For real-world Singapore water-level updates and flood alerts, see [PUB's flood monitoring information](https://www.pub.gov.sg/Public/KeyInitiatives/Flood-Resilience/Flood-Forecasting-and-Monitoring).

## Birds and wind

Birds fly and perch while the Ketapang keeps its steady matte shading. **Wind now** or an automatic gust drops one or two textured leaves. Some fall directly into the river, and a later gust can lift settled leaves off the island. A leaf makes a small ripple and a soft splash when it touches the water. The current carries it downstream until it fades from view.

Select **Enable sound**, then **Wind now** to preview a splash. Wait about nine seconds before sending another gust to see settled leaves lift off the island.

## Explore the forest

- The forest behind the Ketapang has a tall canopy, shorter trees, woody shrubs and ferns. Wildflowers, mushrooms, fallen logs, moss, lichen, lianas and epiphytes add detail at ground and trunk level.
- A small creek flows down the left bank into the river, with wet-bank reeds and local spray. Look for the plantain squirrel, water monitor, frog, butterflies and ground beetles.
- **Soil life** opens a 3D cutaway of litter, topsoil and subsoil, with roots, fungal threads and enlarged microbe symbols.

## Image clarity and 3D assets

The background has 238 taller trees at default quality, with an understory of shorter trees and shrubs. Red and copper Ketapang foliage is mixed among green trees, gold Yellow Flame and pink Trumpet Tree-inspired groves, and low riverbank shrubs. Two 3D mountain ridges wrap around the valley and appear in the river reflections. The main Ketapang has a matte mix of deeper greens, olive and muted copper, with the original leaf veins and bark textures.

The default view uses multisample anti-aliasing, smoothed leaf-mask edges, trilinear mipmaps and up to 16× anisotropic texture filtering. The render buffer follows device pixels up to 2× CSS resolution, subject to a 20-million-pixel budget and the GPU's maximum buffer size. It no longer automatically reduces resolution when the default scene is busy. For example, a 7,000 × 2,000-pixel output fits that budget. The optional `quality=low` mode retains adaptive resolution for slower hardware.

The scene loads all visible 3D meshes and their source materials from the bundled GLBs. Image textures, including the sky panorama and bank surfaces, are embedded in those files. Anti-aliasing smooths edges at the display's native resolution.
